[S1]
INT. THE OLD HOUSE - NIGHT

Dim corridor of an oppressive mid-19th-century dwelling: weathered wood, plaster, shuttered windows, dreadful silence. NARRATOR, adult male in neat ordinary daytime house clothes—simple period shirt, waistcoat, trousers in muted tones—stands composed yet taut, addressing an unseen listener.

(SOUND: house settling, a single distant floorboard creak)

NARRATOR
True!—nervous—very, very dreadfully nervous I had been and am; but why will you say that I am mad?

He touches his ear lightly, eyes sharp.

NARRATOR (CONT'D)
The disease had sharpened my senses—not destroyed—not dulled them. Above all was the sense of hearing acute.

(SOUND: faint high ringing, almost imagined)

NARRATOR (CONT'D)
I heard all things in the heaven and in the earth. I heard many things in hell. How, then, am I mad?

He steadies himself, voice calm and persuasive.

NARRATOR (CONT'D)
Hearken! and observe how healthily—how calmly I can tell you the whole story.


[S2]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - NIGHT

Pitch-dark bedroom: heavy wooden bed, fastened shutters, spare furnishings, visible modest treasures. OLD MAN, elderly, frail, in a plain white nightshirt, lies asleep under covers. Only his face is half-turned; one pale blue eye with a film—the eye of a vulture—is closed.

(SOUND: soft regular breathing, death-watch tick faint in the wall)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
It is impossible to say how first the idea entered my brain; but once conceived, it haunted me day and night.

NARRATOR stands in the doorway in the same neat daytime clothes, staring toward the bed. His blood seems to run cold at the thought of the eye.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Object there was none. Passion there was none. I loved the old man. He had never wronged me. He had never given me insult.

He does not approach the treasures.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
For his gold I had no desire. I think it was his eye! yes, it was this!


[S3]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - DAY

Morning light through unshuttered gaps. OLD MAN sits up in bed, nightshirt neat. NARRATOR, still in neat daytime house clothes, enters boldly with a hearty manner.

(SOUND: floorboards under firm daytime steps, morning birds faint outside)

NARRATOR
(calling him by name, hearty)
How have you passed the night?

OLD MAN answers with a frail nod and murmur of ease. NARRATOR smiles, kind, unsuspicious. He withdraws.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
I was never kinder to the old man than during the whole week before I killed him. Madmen know nothing. But you should have seen me.


[S4]
INT. THE CHAMBER DOOR AND LATCH - NIGHT

Aged wooden door, metal latch. Corridor side dim. NARRATOR now in dark quiet night attire—matte dark coat over simple clothes—holds a closed tin dark lantern. No light escapes it.

(SOUND: latch turns with extreme softness; hinges threaten to creak)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Every night, about midnight, I turned the latch of his door and opened it—oh, so gently!

He opens a crack sufficient only for the lantern and his head. He slides the closed dark lantern through, then thrusts his head in slowly—very, very slowly.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
It took me an hour to place my whole head within the opening so far that I could see him as he lay upon his bed. Ha!—would a madman have been so wise as this?


[S5]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - NIGHT

From the crack: OLD MAN asleep on the heavy bed, eye closed. NARRATOR's head and the closed lantern in the opening. He undoes the lantern cautiously; hinges creak once.

(SOUND: tin hinges creak; single thin ray of light)

A single thin ray falls upon the closed vulture eye. NARRATOR waits. The eye stays shut. He withdraws the lantern, closes the door without a sound.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And this I did for seven long nights—every night just at midnight—but I found the eye always closed; and so it was impossible to do the work; for it was not the old man who vexed me, but his Evil Eye.


[S6]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - DAY

Daybreak. NARRATOR in neat daytime clothes again enters boldly, hearty tone.

(SOUND: cheerful morning footsteps, shutters eased)

NARRATOR
How have you passed the night?

OLD MAN, still in nightshirt, answers without suspicion. NARRATOR's manner is courageously kind.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
So you see he would have been a very profound old man, indeed, to suspect that every night, just at twelve, I looked in upon him while he slept.


[S7]
INT. THE CHAMBER DOOR AND LATCH - NIGHT

Eighth night. NARRATOR in dark night attire, closed dark lantern in hand. He works the latch more slowly than a watch's minute hand.

(SOUND: almost no sound at all; then a tiny metallic slip)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Upon the eighth night I was more than usually cautious in opening the door. Never before that night had I felt the extent of my own powers—of my sagacity.

He chuckles under his breath. Inside, a sudden movement on the bed.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
I fairly chuckled at the idea; and perhaps he heard me; for he moved on the bed suddenly, as if startled.

He keeps pushing the door on steadily. The room beyond is black as pitch—shutters close fastened through fear of robbers. His thumb slips upon the tin fastening of the lantern.


[S8]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - NIGHT

OLD MAN springs up in the heavy bed, nightshirt twisted, pale blue filmy eye wide in the dark.

(SOUND: bedclothes thrash; a startled cry)

OLD MAN
Who's there?

NARRATOR freezes in the opening—head in, lantern still mostly closed. He does not move a muscle. For a long stretch he holds perfectly still.

(SOUND: death-watches ticking in the wall; no other motion)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
I kept quite still and said nothing. For a whole hour I did not move a muscle, and in the meantime I did not hear him lie down.

OLD MAN remains sitting up in the bed, listening—exactly as one hearkens to death-watches in the wall.

Presently a slight groan rises from him: low, stifled, the sound of mortal terror from a soul overcharged with awe.

(SOUND: low stifled groan of terror)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
I knew the sound well. Many a night, just at midnight, when all the world slept, it has welled up from my own bosom. I knew what the old man felt, and pitied him, although I chuckled at heart.

NARRATOR's face in the crack shows pity and private triumph. Death's black shadow seems to envelop the sitting figure.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
He had been trying to fancy them causeless, but could not. All in vain; because Death, in approaching him had stalked with his black shadow before him, and enveloped the victim.

After long patient waiting, NARRATOR opens a very little crevice in the lantern—stealthily.

(SOUND: tin fastening eased; no hinge cry this time)

A single dim ray, like the thread of a spider, shoots from the crevice and falls full upon the vulture eye.

The eye is open—wide, wide open. Dull blue, with a hideous veil. Nothing else of the face is lit.

(SOUND: low dull quick sound, like a watch enveloped in cotton)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And have I not told you that what you mistake for madness is but over-acuteness of the sense?—now, I say, there came to my ears a low, dull, quick sound, such as a watch makes when enveloped in cotton.

NARRATOR's fury rises. He scarcely breathes. He holds the lantern motionless, ray steady on the eye. The hellish tattoo grows quicker and louder.

(SOUND: heartbeat-like tattoo quickening, louder under the silence of the old house)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
It was the beating of the old man's heart. It increased my fury, as the beating of a drum stimulates the soldier into courage.

He still refrains. The sound grows louder every instant. A new anxiety seizes him—the sound would be heard by a neighbour.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
The old man's hour had come!

With a loud yell he throws open the lantern. Sudden full beam floods the chamber. He leaps into the room.

(SOUND: yell; lantern shutter slams open; bedclothes tear; one single shriek)

OLD MAN shrieks once—once only. NARRATOR drags him to the floor and pulls the heavy bed over him.

NARRATOR smiles gaily in the lantern light. For many minutes the heart beats on with a muffled sound beneath the bed.

(SOUND: muffled heartbeat under heavy wood and bedding; then silence)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
At length it ceased. The old man was dead.

He removes the bed. Examines the corpse. Places his hand upon the heart and holds it there many minutes. No pulsation. Stone, stone dead.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
His eye would trouble me no more.


[S9]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - NIGHT (LATER)

Night waning. Lantern light. NARRATOR, still in night attire now slightly disordered from labor, works hastily but in silence. He dismembers the corpse—cuts off the head and the arms and the legs—clinical, not lingered upon. A tub stands ready off the main floor and catches all.

(SOUND: soft wet work; no cry; tub receives what falls)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
If still you think me mad, you will think so no longer when I describe the wise precautions I took for the concealment of the body.

He takes up three planks from the flooring, deposits all between the scantlings, then replaces the boards so cleverly, so cunningly, that no human eye could detect anything wrong. No stain, no blood-spot on the boards.

(SOUND: planks lifted and seated flush; soft scrape of wood on wood)

NARRATOR
(soft, to himself)
A tub had caught all—ha! ha!

It is four o'clock—still dark as midnight. A bell sounds the hour.

(SOUND: distant bell striking four; then a firm knocking at a lower door)


[S10]
INT. THE STREET DOOR - NIGHT

Heavy period street door of the old house in pre-dawn dark. NARRATOR comes down with a light heart, clothes ordered enough to welcome guests, no visible stain. He opens.

(SOUND: knocking ceases; door latch; night air)

Three men enter: OFFICER REYNOLDS, OFFICER CLEMM, and OFFICER HARTWELL—adult men in matching dark mid-19th-century civic coats, neat and authoritative, with restrained early-police markers. They introduce themselves with perfect suavity.

OFFICER REYNOLDS
Officers of the police. A shriek was heard by a neighbour during the night. Suspicion of foul play. We are to search the premises.

NARRATOR smiles—what has he now to fear?

NARRATOR
The shriek was my own in a dream. The old man is absent in the country. Come in. Search—search well.


[S11]
INT. THE OLD HOUSE - NIGHT

NARRATOR leads the three officers through quiet corridors and modest rooms of the old house, lantern or candle light moving with them. His manner is singularly at ease.

(SOUND: multiple footsteps on old boards; polite low voices)

He shows them rooms without disturbance. They remain composed, convinced by his confidence.


[S12]
INT. THE OLD MAN'S CHAMBER - NIGHT

NARRATOR leads them at length into the chamber. Treasures secure, undisturbed. Heavy bed restored. Floorboards innocent. In the wild audacity of his triumph he brings chairs in and seats the officers. He places his own seat upon the very spot beneath which the corpse reposes.

(SOUND: chairs set down; pleasant room tone)

OFFICER CLEMM and OFFICER HARTWELL sit. OFFICER REYNOLDS remains at ease. They chat of familiar things. NARRATOR answers cheerily.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
The officers were satisfied. My manner had convinced them. I was singularly at ease.

Ere long NARRATOR grows pale. He wishes them gone. His head aches. He fancies a ringing in his ears.

(SOUND: faint ringing that grows more distinct)

The ringing becomes more distinct—until he finds the noise is not within his ears. It is a low, dull, quick sound—much such a sound as a watch makes when enveloped in cotton.

(SOUND: low dull quick heartbeat-like tattoo, rising under the chat)

NARRATOR gasps for breath. The officers hear it not. They chat on pleasantly and smile.

NARRATOR talks more freely, then more quickly, more vehemently. He rises and argues about trifles in a high key with violent gesticulations.

(SOUND: heartbeat tattoo steadily increasing; chair legs scrape)

NARRATOR
(agitated, high)
Why will you not be gone?

He paces the floor to and fro with heavy strides. He foams, raves, swears. He swings the chair upon which he had been sitting and grates it upon the boards.

(SOUND: chair grated hard on boards; heartbeat louder over all)

The noise grows louder—louder—louder. Still the men chat pleasantly and smile.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Was it possible they heard not? Almighty God!—no, no! They heard!—they suspected!—they knew!—they were making a mockery of my horror!

He cannot bear the hypocritical smiles. Agony. He must scream or die.

(SOUND: heartbeat thunderous now; his breath ragged)

NARRATOR
(shrieking)
Villains! dissemble no more! I admit the deed!—tear up the planks!—here, here!—It is the beating of his hideous heart!

He points wildly at the very spot beneath his chair. The three officers freeze, smiles gone, staring at the floorboards and at him.

(SOUND: final shriek echoing; then the tattoo seems to fill the chamber; dreadful silence under it)

FADE OUT.

THE END
