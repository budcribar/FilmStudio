[S1]
EXT. JUDGE MILLER'S PLACE, SANTA CLARA VALLEY - DAY

Sun-kissed lawns, gravelled driveways under tall poplars, a wide cool veranda. BUCK — large St. Bernard–Scotch shepherd mix, one hundred forty pounds, massive chest, heavy forelegs, thick glossy coat with stray brown on muzzle and above eyes and a splash of white midmost down the chest — stalks imperiously among fox terriers.

(SOUND: birdsong, distant stable horses, terriers yelping)

Buck plunges into the big cement tank, shakes water in a glittering spray, then escorts two young women along a twilight path. At the rear: great stables, vine-clad cottages, orchards, berry patches.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Buck did not read the newspapers, or he would have known that trouble was brewing... for every tide-water dog, strong of muscle and with warm, long hair.


[S2]
INT. JUDGE MILLER'S LIBRARY - NIGHT

A roaring library fire. JUDGE MILLER, prosperous country gentleman in dark wool, sits with Buck at his feet. Buck carries himself in right royal fashion.

(SOUND: fire crackle, wind at the windows)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
During the four years since his puppyhood he had lived the life of a sated aristocrat. But hunting and kindred outdoor delights had kept down the fat and hardened his muscles.


[S3]
EXT. JUDGE MILLER'S PLACE, SANTA CLARA VALLEY - NIGHT

Orchard shadows. MANUEL, gardener's helper in dusty work clothes and cap, doubles a stout rope around Buck's neck under the collar. A solitary stranger waits. Money chinks between them.

(SOUND: night insects, rope fibers creaking, coins)

STRANGER
You might wrap up the goods before you deliver 'm.

MANUEL
Twist it, an' you'll choke 'm plentee.

Buck accepts the rope with quiet dignity — then growls as the ends pass to the stranger. The rope tightens. Buck springs; the man grapples his throat, throws him on his back. Buck's tongue lolls; his great chest pants. Strength ebbs. Eyes glaze.

They flag the train at the little flag station and throw him into the baggage car.


[S4]
EXT. COLLEGE PARK FLAG STATION - NIGHT

The baggage car door slams. The locomotive shrieks a crossing.

(SOUND: locomotive whistle, coupling iron, wheels gathering speed)


[S5]
INT. BAGGAGE CAR / EXT. SAN FRANCISCO WATER FRONT - NIGHT

Buck's eyes open — unbridled anger of a kidnapped king. He closes jaws on the man's hand until choked senseless again.

In a little shed back of a saloon, the kidnapper's hand is wrapped in a bloody handkerchief; trouser leg ripped knee to ankle. Four men file the heavy brass collar from Buck's neck and fling him into a cagelike crate.

(SOUND: file on brass, crate wood slamming, saloon piano distant)

SALOON-KEEPER
That makes a hundred and fifty... and he's worth it, or I'm a squarehead.

Buck lies nursing wrath and wounded pride. The saloon-keeper's bulging face peers in by tallow candle. Buck's joyful bark twists into a savage growl.


[S6]
EXT. SEATTLE BACK YARD - DAY

Small high-walled back yard. Four men gingerly carry the crate in. MAN IN THE RED SWEATER — stout, red sweater sagging generously at the neck — signs the book, brings hatchet and club.

(SOUND: hatchet biting crate wood, men scrambling onto wall perches)

MAN IN THE RED SWEATER
Now, you red-eyed devil.

Buck — coat matted, eyes blood-shot, frothing — launches one hundred forty pounds of fury. In mid-air the club checks him; teeth clip agonizingly. He charges a dozen times; the club smashes him down. Blood flows from nose, mouth, ears; coat sprayed and flecked with bloody slaver. A frightful blow on the nose. He hurls himself again; the man catches the under jaw and wrenches. Buck crashes on head and chest. Final shrewd blow — senseless.

MAN IN THE RED SWEATER
Answers to the name of Buck. Well, Buck, my boy, we've had our little ruction... You've learned your place, and I know mine. Be a good dog and all 'll go well... Be a bad dog, and I'll whale the stuffin' outa you. Understand?

He pats the head he pounded. Buck's hair bristles but he endures. He drinks eagerly, bolts raw meat chunk by chunk from the man's hand.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
He was beaten (he knew that); but he was not broken. That club was a revelation. A man with a club was a lawgiver, a master to be obeyed, though not necessarily conciliated.

Other dogs arrive in crates. Money passes. Strangers take dogs away. Buck is glad each time he is not selected.


[S7]
EXT. SEATTLE BACK YARD - DAY

PERRAULT — little weazened French-Canadian, swarthy — spatters broken English. FRANCOIS — black-faced giant, French-Canadian half-breed, twice as swarthy — waits.

(SOUND: street wagon, distant harbor)

PERRAULT
Sacredam! Dat one dam bully dog! Eh? How moch?

MAN IN THE RED SWEATER
Three hundred, and a present at that. And seem' it's government money, you ain't got no kick coming, eh, Perrault?

Perrault grins. Buck and CURLY, a good-natured Newfoundland, are led away.


[S8]
EXT. NARWHAL SHIP - DECK AND 'TWEEN-DECKS - DAY

Receding Seattle. Buck and Curly turned over to Francois below. They join SPITZ — big snow-white fellow, smiling treacherously — and DAVE — gloomy, morose wheeler.

(SOUND: propeller throb, hull creak, Queen Charlotte Sound roll)

Spitz steals from Buck's food. Buck springs; Francois's whip sings first. Fair, Buck decides.

Day and night the ship throbs colder. One morning the propeller stills. Francois leashes them on deck. Buck's feet sink into white mushy stuff. He snorts, sniffs, licks — it bites like fire and is gone. Onlookers laugh. His first snow.


[S9]
EXT. DYEA BEACH - DAY

Nightmare confusion. Camp near the log store. Curly makes friendly advances to a husky the size of a full-grown wolf. No warning — leap, metallic clip of teeth, Curly's face ripped open eye to jaw.

(SOUND: snarling circle, yelps, axe haft, clubs on ribs)

Thirty or forty huskies surround them in silent intent circle, licking chops. Curly tumbles; they bury her screaming under bristling mass. Francois swings an axe; three men with clubs scatter them. Two minutes — she lies limp and lifeless in bloody trampled snow. Spitz runs out his scarlet tongue and laughs. Buck hates him with bitter deathless hatred.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
So that was the way. No fair play. Once down, that was the end of you. Well, he would see to it that he never went down.


[S10]
EXT. DYEA BEACH - DAY

Francois fastens straps and buckles on Buck — a harness. Dignity sorely hurt, Buck is too wise to rebel. He hauls Francois on a sled for firewood. Dave nips his hindquarters; Spitz growls reproof or jerks the traces. Buck learns: stop at "ho," go at "mush," swing wide on bends.

(SOUND: sled runners, whip crack, harness jingle)

FRANCOIS
T'ree vair' good dogs. Dat Buck, heem pool lak hell. I tich heem queek as anyt'ing.

Perrault brings BILLEE and JOE — husky brothers. Billee excessively good-natured; Joe sour, perpetual snarl, malignant eye. Spitz thrashes Billee; Joe whirls, mane bristling, eyes diabolical — Spitz forgoes him. SOL-LEKS arrives: old, long, lean, gaunt, battle-scarred, single eye. Sol-leks the Angry One. Buck approaches his blind side; Sol-leks slashes shoulder to the bone. Forever after Buck avoids that side.


[S11]
EXT. DYEA BEACH CAMP - NIGHT

Tent glows with candle. Buck enters; Perrault and Francois bombard him with curses and cooking utensils. He flees into outer cold. Chill wind nips his wounded shoulder. He wanders among tents; savage dogs rush; he bristles and snarls; they let him pass.

(SOUND: wind bite, distant dog fights, snow crunch)

He finds the team gone — then sinks through snow onto Billee curled in a snug ball. Warm air. Billee licks his face. Buck digs his own hole with fuss and waste effort. Heat of his body fills the space. He sleeps, growling at bad dreams.

Morning: snow completely buries him. Fear of the trap — wild thing. Muscles contract; hair stands; he bounds straight up into blinding day, snow flying in a flashing cloud. He remembers the camp.

FRANCOIS
Wot I say? Dat Buck for sure learn queek as anyt'ing.


[S12]
EXT. DYEA CANON / CHILCOOT DIVIDE - DAY

Nine dogs in harness swinging up the trail. Dark starts, gray dawn miles, camp after dark. Pound and a half of sun-dried salmon — never enough. Buck loses fastidiousness; mates rob unfinished ration. He eats as fast as they. Copies PIKE stealing bacon; Dub the awkward blunderer is punished for Buck's misdeed.

(SOUND: traces creak, ice under paws, wind over divide)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
This first theft marked Buck as fit to survive... It marked the decay or going to pieces of his moral nature, a vain thing and a handicap in the ruthless struggle for existence.

Muscles hard as iron. He bites ice from between toes; breaks water-hole scum with stiff forelegs. Instincts long dead alive again. On still cold nights he points his nose at a star and howls long and wolflike.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And his cadences were their cadences... Thus... he came into his own again; and he came because men had found a yellow metal in the North, and because Manuel was a gardener's helper whose wages did not lap over the needs of his wife and divers small copies of himself.


[S13]
EXT. LAKE LE BARGE - NIGHT

Bleak miserable camp. Perpendicular rock wall. Fire and sleeping robes on the lake ice; tent discarded. Driving snow, wind like a white-hot knife. Buck's snug nest under the rock — Spitz occupies it. Warning snarl. The beast in Buck roars. They shoot out in a tangle.

(SOUND: wind knife, snarl, sudden oath, club on bone, yelp)

FRANCOIS
A-a-ah! Gif it to heem, by Gar! Gif it to heem, the dirty t'eef!

Pandemonium — starving huskies, four or five score, skulking furry forms. Grub-box capsized. Clubs fall unheeded. Team-dogs burst from nests and are set upon. Skeleton huskies with blazing eyes. Buck beset by three; head and shoulders ripped. Spitz treacherously attacks from the side. The team flees onto the lake ice. Later they shelter in forest — every one wounded. Half the grub gone. Even moccasins and whip-lash eaten.


[S14]
EXT. THIRTY MILE RIVER - DAY

Wild water defies frost. Six days exhausting toil. Perrault breaks through ice bridges, saved by long pole; builds fire each time at fifty below. Sled breaks through with Dave and Buck — half-frozen, coated solidly with ice, run around fire till singed. Spitz goes through; Buck and Dave strain backward; Francois pulls till tendons crack. Rim ice breaks; dogs hoisted by rope up a cliff.

(SOUND: ice crack and roar, pole splash, fire spit, teeth chattering)

Buck's feet soft from generations of civilization. He limps in agony. Francois rubs them half an hour each night; sacrifices moccasin tops to make four for Buck. One morning Buck lies on his back, four feet waving, refuses to budge without them. Perrault's weazened face twists into a grin.

At the Pelly, DOLLY goes suddenly mad — long heartbreaking wolf howl, springs for Buck. He races in terror, she one leap behind, frothing. Francois's axe crashes on mad Dolly's head. Exhausted Buck — Spitz springs, teeth sink twice to the bone. Francois's lash gives Spitz the worst whipping yet.

PERRAULT
One devil, dat Spitz. Some dam day heem keel dat Buck.

FRANCOIS
Dat Buck two devils... some dam fine day heem get mad lak hell an' den heem chew dat Spitz all up an' spit heem out on de snow. Sure. I know.


[S15]
EXT. YUKON TRAIL TOWARD DAWSON - DAY

War. Buck comes between Spitz and the shirks. Shields Pike hidden under snow. Covert mutiny; general insubordination. Continual bickering. Francois's lash always singing; small avail. Buck works faithfully yet slyly precipitates fights.

(SOUND: whip song, dog quarrel, sled bells, frost crunch)

They pull into Dawson. Countless dogs haul cabin logs and freight. Nightly at nine, twelve, three — nocturnal song, weird eerie chant. Buck joins. Aurora borealis flames coldly; stars leap in frost dance.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
It was an old song, old as the breed itself... invested with the woe of unnumbered generations... And that he should be stirred by it marked the completeness with which he harked back through the ages of fire and roof to the raw beginnings of life in the howling ages.


[S16]
EXT. FROZEN CREEK UNDER AURORA - NIGHT

After supper Dub turns up a snowshoe rabbit and misses. Whole team in full cry. Northwest Police camp — fifty huskies join. Sixty strong. Buck leads, splendid body flashing leap by leap in wan white moonlight. Rabbit a pale frost wraith.

(SOUND: pack cry, snow plough, Buck's eager whine, aurora silence under it)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
There is an ecstasy that marks the summit of life... This ecstasy... came to Buck, leading the pack, sounding the old wolf-cry... He was sounding the deeps of his nature... going back into the womb of Time.

Spitz cuts across a narrow neck. Rabbit shrieks as white teeth break its back. Hell's chorus of delight. Buck drives in upon Spitz shoulder to shoulder. They roll. Time has come — to the death. Silent wolfish circle waits. Fang clashes fang. Buck streams blood. He possesses imagination. Rushes as if for the shoulder trick, sweeps low — teeth close on Spitz's left foreleg. Crunch of breaking bone. Thrice more; right foreleg breaks. Spitz on three legs. Circle tightens. Buck springs; shoulder meets shoulder. Dark circle becomes a dot on moon-flooded snow as Spitz disappears.

Buck stands — successful champion, the dominant primordial beast who had made his kill and found it good.


[S17]
EXT. CAMP ON TRAIL - DAY

Francois discovers Spitz missing; Buck covered with wounds.

(SOUND: fire snap, harness buckles)

FRANCOIS
Eh? Wot I say? I spik true w'en I say dat Buck two devils.

PERRAULT
Dat Spitz fight lak hell.

FRANCOIS
An' dat Buck fight lak two hells. An' now we make good time. No more Spitz, no more trouble, sure.

Francois harnesses; brings Sol-leks to lead. Buck springs upon Sol-leks, drives him back, stands in his place. Francois drags Buck aside; Buck displaces Sol-leks again. Francois comes with heavy club. Buck retreats slowly, circles beyond range, watches the club — wise in the way of clubs. Open revolt. He wants leadership. They run him about an hour; throw clubs; curse him. He answers snarl for curse, keeps out of reach, advertises plainly: when desire is met, he will come in.

Perrault looks at his watch and swears. Francois throws down the club.

PERRAULT
T'row down de club.

Buck trots in, laughing triumphantly, swings into position at the head of the team. Traces fastened. Sled broken out. They dash onto the river trail.


[S18]
EXT. YUKON TRAIL - RECORD RUN - DAY

Buck takes up duties of leadership. Superior even of Spitz where judgment and quick acting required. Licks the team into shape. Pike shaken for loafing; Joe smothered till he whines for mercy. Solidarity returns; dogs leap as one. Teek and Koona added; Buck breaks them in with celerity.

(SOUND: runners singing on hard trail, whip infrequent, men running by turn)

FRANCOIS
Nevaire such a dog as dat Buck! No, nevaire! Heem worth one t'ousan' dollair, by Gar!

Fourteen days averaging forty miles. Thirty Mile covered in one day going out. Sixty-mile dash. Across lakes they fly so fast the running man tows behind on a rope. Last night of second week: White Pass, lights of Skaguay and shipping at their feet.


[S19]
EXT. SKAGUAY MAIN STREET - DAY

Record run. Worshipful crowd of dog-busters and mushers. Francois calls Buck, throws arms around him, weeps. Official orders. Last of Francois and Perrault. They pass out of Buck's life for good.

(SOUND: street crowd, dog bells, Francois's rough sob)

A Scotch half-breed takes charge. Mail train — heavy toil, heavy load, word from the world to men who seek gold under the shadow of the Pole.


[S20]
EXT. MAIL TRAIL / CAMPFIRES - NIGHT

Monotonous machine-like regularity. Buck masters fierce camp dogs in three battles. Best of all he loves to lie near the fire, hind legs crouched, fore legs out, head raised, eyes blinking dreamily at flames.

(SOUND: fire, distant husky song, wind in timber)

Sometimes the flames are of another fire. HAIRY MAN — shorter of leg, longer of arm, stringy knotty muscles, long matted hair, head slanted back, all but naked in ragged fire-scorched skin, much body hair matted to thick fur, trunk inclined forward, catlike springiness — clutches a stick with heavy stone. Peers fearfully into darkness. Beyond the fire, gleaming coals two by two — eyes of great beasts. Hair rises on Buck's back; he whimpers or growls soft.

HALF-BREED COOK
Hey, you Buck, wake up!

The other world vanishes.

Dave sickens. Something wrong inside. Falls repeatedly. Taken out; he resents it, attacks Sol-leks, bites through traces, stands in his proper place. They harness him again. He holds out to camp. Morning too weak. Worms forward hitching. Last they see him gasping in snow, yearning. Train halted. Scotch half-breed retraces steps. Revolver-shot. Whips snap; bells tinkle; sleds churn. Buck knows, and every dog knows, what took place behind the belt of river trees.


[S21]
EXT. SKAGUAY MAIN STREET - DAY

Salt Water Mail arrives. Dogs wretched. Buck one hundred forty pounds dwindled to one hundred fifteen. Footsore, dead tired — slow prolonged strength drainage of months. Fresh Hudson Bay dogs to replace the worthless. Sold.

(SOUND: tottering paws on street boards, Hal's cartridge belt creak)

HAL — youngster nineteen or twenty, big Colt's revolver and hunting-knife on belt bristling with cartridges — and CHARLES — middle-aged, lightish, weak watery eyes, mustache twisted fiercely up over limply drooping lip — buy them harness and all for a song. MERCEDES — Charles's wife, Hal's sister, pretty and soft — flutters.

Camp slipshod: tent half stretched, dishes unwashed, disorder. Clothes-sack argued front then back. Three neighboring men grin and wink.

NEIGHBOR
You've got a right smart load as it is... I wouldn't tote that tent along if I was you.

MERCEDES
Undreamed of! However in the world could I manage without a tent?


[S22]
EXT. SKAGUAY MAIN STREET - DAY

Fourteen dogs — six Outside added (pointers, Newfoundland, mongrels). Overload. Dogs unable to move sled. Hal prepares to lash.

(SOUND: whip whistle, Mercedes's cry, runners frozen groan)

MERCEDES
Oh, Hal, you mustn't... The poor dears!

NEIGHBOR
...you can help them a mighty lot by breaking out that sled. The runners are froze fast.

Hal breaks runners out. Top-heavy sled forges, swings on turn, goes over, spills half its load. Dogs angry; Buck raging breaks into a run. Hal tripped, capsized sled grinds over him. Outfit scatters along chief thoroughfare. Kind-hearted citizens catch dogs, gather belongings, advise: half the load and twice the dogs.


[S23]
EXT. TRAIL FROM SKAGUAY TOWARD DAWSON - DAY

Incompetent trail. Half the night to pitch slovenly camp; half morning to break it. Some days not ten miles. Overfeeding then underfeeding. Outside dogs die. Dub shot with the big Colt's. Billee axed in the traces. Horse-hide traded for revolver — strips of galvanized iron thawing into leathery strings and hair.

(SOUND: club on ribs, family quarrel, Mercedes weeping, traces dragging)

Family quarrel unending — fathers, mothers, uncles, art, politics. Mercedes rides, one hundred twenty pounds, last straw. Legs go limp like spoiled child when they try to make her walk. Seven skeletons left. Buck at the head, blind with weakness half the time, keeping trail by loom of it and dim feel of feet. Coat limp, draggled, matted with dried blood; ribs outlined through loose wrinkled hide.


[S24]
EXT. WHITE RIVER MOUTH (THORNTON'S CAMP) - DAY

Beautiful spring weather — sap rising, birds, wild-fowl wedges — but neither dogs nor humans aware. They stagger into camp. JOHN THORNTON whittles last touches on a birch axe-handle. Dogs drop as though struck dead.

(SOUND: running spring water, bird song, Hal's whip, club impacts)

HAL
They told us up above that the bottom was dropping out of the trail... and here we are.

JOHN THORNTON
And they told you true. The bottom's likely to drop out at any moment. Only fools, with the blind luck of fools, could have made it.

Hal uncoils whip. Team does not rise. Blows. Sol-leks crawls up; Teek; Joe yelping; Pike on third try. Buck makes no effort. Lash again and again. He has made up his mind not to get up. Vague impending doom — thin rotten ice all day. Spark of life flickers nearly out. Numb. Club impacts heard faintly, as from a great distance.

John Thornton springs with an inarticulate animal cry. Hal hurled backward. Mercedes screams. Charles looks on wistfully, too stiff to rise.

JOHN THORNTON
If you strike that dog again, I'll kill you.

HAL
It's my dog... Get out of my way, or I'll fix you. I'm going to Dawson.

Hal draws long hunting-knife. Thornton raps knuckles with axe-handle; knife drops. Stoops, picks it up, cuts Buck's traces with two strokes.

A few minutes later the others pull out — Pike leading, Mercedes riding, Hal at gee-pole, Charles stumbling rear. Buck raises head. Sled's back end drops as into a rut; gee-pole jerks; Mercedes screams; Charles turns; whole section of ice gives way. Dogs and humans disappear. Yawning hole. Bottom dropped out of the trail.

John Thornton and Buck look at each other.

JOHN THORNTON
You poor devil.

Buck licks his hand.


[S25]
EXT. WHITE RIVER MOUTH (THORNTON'S CAMP) - DAY

Long spring days. Buck slowly wins back strength. SKEET — little Irish setter — washes and cleanses his wounds each morning like a mother cat. NIG — huge black dog, half bloodhound half deerhound, eyes that laugh — friendly, less demonstrative. No jealousy. They entice him into ridiculous games. Thornton joins.

(SOUND: river run, bird hum, Skeet's soft tongue on wounds, Thornton's rough laugh)

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Love, genuine passionate love, was his for the first time... love that was feverish and burning, that was adoration, that was madness, it had taken John Thornton to arouse.

Thornton takes Buck's head roughly between his hands, rests his own head upon Buck's, shakes him back and forth, calling ill names that to Buck are love names. Buck's ecstasy. Released, he springs up, mouth laughing, eyes eloquent, throat vibrant — remains without movement.

JOHN THORNTON
God! you can all but speak!

Buck seizes Thornton's hand in his mouth, closes so fiercely flesh bears teeth impress — feigned bite for a caress. For the most part he adores at a distance, lying by the hour at Thornton's feet, studying each fleeting expression. Often the strength of his gaze draws Thornton's head around; they return the gaze without speech, hearts shining out of eyes.

Buck will not let Thornton out of sight. Even at night he creeps to the tent flap to listen to his master's breathing. Fear that Thornton will pass out of his life as the others did.


[S26]
EXT. WHITE RIVER CAMP / CIRCLE CITY BAR / FORTY-MILE CREEK RAPIDS - DAY

Primitive strain remains alive. Buck steals from any other man, any other camp — never from Thornton. Strange dogs swiftly acknowledge supremacy or struggle for life. Kill or be killed. Mercy a weakness.

(SOUND: bar crowd, fist impact, Buck's roar neither bark nor yelp)

At Circle City, Black Burton strikes Thornton without warning. Buck leaves the floor for Burton's throat. Arm blocks; Buck drives in again; throat torn open. Miners' meeting: sufficient provocation. Reputation made through every camp in Alaska.

Later: lining a long narrow poling-boat down bad rapids on Forty-Mile Creek. Hans and Pete snub with thin Manila rope. Boat flirts over; Thornton flung toward worst wild water no swimmer could live. Buck springs in; overhauls him; Thornton grasps tail. Shore impossible. Thornton clutches slippery rock.

JOHN THORNTON
Go, Buck! Go!

Buck turns obediently, is dragged ashore. Rope attached to neck and shoulders; launched again. Miscalculates; jerked under. Half drowned; breath pounded in, water out. Master's voice like electric shock. Third launch — straight. Strikes Thornton like battering ram. Arms close around shaggy neck. Jerked under; drag over jagged bottom; veer to bank. Three broken ribs. They camp till Buck can travel.


[S27]
EXT. ELDORADO SALOON, DAWSON - DAY

Winter. Sixty below. Boastful talk of favorite dogs. Matthewson, Bonanza King, slams a sack of gold dust the size of a bologna sausage on the bar.

(SOUND: saloon din, gold-dust sack thud, street cold silence outside)

MATTHEWSON
I've got a thousand dollars that says he can't. And there it is.

Thornton has no thousand. Jim O'Brien lends. Outside: sled with twenty fifty-pound sacks of flour, runners frozen fast. Odds three to one against. Partners rake two hundred against Matthewson's six hundred. Ten-dog team unhitched. Buck in own harness — perfect condition, one hundred fifty pounds grit and virility, coat sheen of silk, mane half bristled.

Thornton kneels, head in hands, cheek on cheek. Whispers.

JOHN THORNTON
As you love me, Buck. As you love me.

Buck whines suppressed eagerness. Seizes mittened hand, presses teeth, releases slowly. Thornton steps back.

JOHN THORNTON
Now, Buck.

Buck tightens traces, slacks several inches.

JOHN THORNTON
Gee!

Plunge right — crisp crackling under runners.

JOHN THORNTON
Haw!

Left — snapping; sled pivots; runners slip inches. Broken out.

JOHN THORNTON
Now, MUSH!

Pistol-shot command. Buck throws himself forward. Muscles writhe and knot like live things. Chest low, head forward and down, feet flying, claws scarring hard-packed snow in parallel grooves. Sled sways, half-starts... half inch... inch... two inches... jerks diminish... steadily along. Thornton runs behind with short cheery words. Hundred yards to firewood pile. Cheer grows to roar. Hats and mittens flying.

Thornton falls on knees beside Buck, head against head, shaking him, cursing him long and fervently and softly and lovingly. Skookum Bench king offers twelve hundred.

JOHN THORNTON
Sir, no, sir. You can go to hell, sir. It's the best I can do for you, sir.

Buck seizes Thornton's hand in his teeth. Onlookers draw back to respectful distance.


[S28]
EXT. STEWART RIVER / EASTERN WILDERNESS - DAY

Sixteen hundred dollars in five minutes. Debts paid. Journey east after fabled lost mine. Seventy miles up Yukon, left into Stewart, past Mayo and McQuestion, till Stewart a streamlet among upstanding peaks — backbone of the continent. Indian fashion: handful of salt and rifle; hunt dinner in day's travel. Straight meat; time-card drawn on limitless future.

(SOUND: sled then pack creak, rifle shot distant, summer blizzard wind, gnat hum, glacier drip)

Boundless delight for Buck — hunting, fishing, indefinite wandering. Seasons wheel: summer blizzards on naked mountains; swarming gnats in valleys; strawberries in glacier shadows; weird sad lake country; another winter on obliterated trails. Ancient blazed path that begins nowhere and ends nowhere. Time-graven wreckage of hunting lodge; long-barrelled flint-lock.

Spring: not the Lost Cabin, but a shallow placer in a broad valley. Gold like yellow butter across the washing-pan. Each day thousands of dollars. Gold sacked in moose-hide bags, fifty pounds to the bag, piled like firewood outside the spruce-bough lodge.


[S29]
EXT. LOST CABIN COUNTRY / PLACER VALLEY - NIGHT

Nothing for the dogs but hauling meat. Buck muses by the fire. Hairy-man visions more frequent. Call sounding in the depths of the forest — mysteriously thrilling and luring. Compelled to turn back upon the fire and plunge into forest. Love for John Thornton draws him back.

(SOUND: fire, forest night, long-drawn howl unlike husky)

One night he springs from sleep, mane bristling. From the forest: long-drawn howl. He dashes through woods to an open place. Erect on haunches, nose to sky — long lean timber wolf. WILD BROTHER.

Buck stalks in, half crouching, every movement threatening and friendly. Wolf flees. Chase. Cornered at timber jam; whirls snarling. Buck circles with friendly advances. At last they sniff noses. Play in nervous half-coy way. Wolf starts at easy lope; makes clear Buck is to come. Side by side through sombre twilight, up creek, across bleak divide, into level forest country. Buck wildly glad — answering the call, running by wood brother.

They stop to drink. Buck remembers John Thornton. Sits. Wolf encourages; Buck turns back. Wild brother runs beside him whining softly, then sits, points nose up, howls mournful. Howl grows faint and is lost.

Buck dashes into camp, springs upon Thornton in frenzy of affection — overturning, scrambling, licking, biting hand.

JOHN THORNTON
Playing the general tom-fool.

For two days Buck never leaves camp. Then call sounds more imperiously. Restlessness. Sleeps out. Crosses divide; wanders a week. Kills black bear blinded by mosquitoes. Scatters wolverenes from the kill.


[S30]
EXT. DIVIDE AND FOREST - DAY

Blood-longing stronger. Killer, thing that preyed, surviving triumphantly. Great pride. Coat glorious; might be mistaken for gigantic wolf but for brown muzzle splashes and white chest rift. Cunning of wolf plus shepherd and St. Bernard intelligence plus fiercest school. Life streams in splendid flood.

(SOUND: moose herd, antlers toss, Buck's bark and dance, four days of pursuit)

Band of twenty moose; great bull over six feet, palmated antlers fourteen points, feathered arrow in flank. Buck cuts him out. Patience of the wild — dogged, tireless. Half a day; young bulls aid less and less. Twilight: old bull stands lowered head as mates shamble on. Three hundredweight more than half a ton. Night and day Buck never leaves him — no browse, no drink. At end of fourth day pulls the great moose down. Eats and sleeps a day and night. Then long easy lope toward camp and John Thornton.

Sense of calamity. Fresh trail — neck hair rippling. Pregnant silence. Birds flitted; squirrels hiding. Finds Nig dead, arrow protruding head and feathers either side. Sled-dog thrashing in death-struggle. Faint sing-song chant from camp. Hans lying on face, feathered with arrows like a porcupine. Wreckage of spruce-bough lodge. Gust of overpowering rage. For the last time passion usurps cunning — because of great love for John Thornton.


[S31]
EXT. SPRUCE-BOUGH LODGE CAMP - DAY

YEEHATS dancing about wreckage. Fearful roaring. Buck a live hurricane of fury. Springs at chief, rips throat wide; jugular spouts. Next bound another throat. Plunges in their midst, tearing, rending, destroying — so rapid they shoot one another; spear meant for Buck drives through another hunter. Panic. They flee proclaiming advent of the Evil Spirit. Buck drags them down like deer among the trees.

(SOUND: chant breaking to terror cries, throat rips, arrows hiss, spear thud, fleeing feet)

Wearying, he returns. Pete killed in blankets in first surprise. Thornton's desperate struggle fresh-written on earth to edge of deep pool. Skeet by the edge, head and fore feet in water, faithful to the last. Pool muddy from sluice boxes hides what it contains. Buck follows trace into water — no trace leads away.

All day he broodes by the pool or roams. Death he knows. Great void akin to hunger that food cannot fill. At times, contemplating Yeehat carcasses, great pride — he has killed man, noblest game of all, in face of law of club and fang. Thenceforward unafraid of them except when they bear arrows, spears, and clubs.


[S32]
EXT. MOONLIT CLEARING AND WOLF COUNTRY - NIGHT

Full moon rises high; land bathed in ghostly day. Stirring of new life. Faint sharp yelp; chorus. Pack pours into moonlight clearing in silvery flood. Buck motionless as statue in the center. Boldest leaps; Buck strikes, breaks neck. Three others; draw back streaming blood. Whole pack forward pell-mell. Buck pivots on hind legs, snapping and gashing, everywhere at once. Forced back to creek bed, high gravel bank angle — protected three sides.

(SOUND: pack yelps, neck break, lolling tongues, water lap, then long wolf howl)

Half hour; wolves draw back discomfited. One long lean gray advances cautiously, friendly — wild brother. They whine, touch noses. Old wolf, gaunt and battle-scarred, sniffs noses. Sits, points nose at moon, breaks out long wolf howl. Others sit and howl. Call comes to Buck in unmistakable accents. He too sits and howls. Comes out of angle. Pack crowds around, sniffing half-friendly, half-savage. Leaders lift the yelp; spring into woods. Wolves swing in behind. Buck runs with them, side by side with the wild brother, yelping as he runs.


[S33]
EXT. MOONLIT CLEARING AND WOLF COUNTRY / GOLD-STREAM VALLEY - NIGHT AND DAY

Years not many. Yeehats note change in timber-wolf breed — splashes of brown on head and muzzle, rift of white centring down chest. They tell of a Ghost Dog that runs at the head of the pack. Afraid — cunning greater than they; steals from camps, robs traps, slays dogs, defies bravest hunters. Hunters fail to return; some found with throats slashed cruelly open and wolf prints greater than any wolf. A certain valley they never enter.

(SOUND: pack song under pale moonlight or glimmering borealis; summer silence then one long mournful howl)

In summers one visitor the Yeehats do not know. A great, gloriously coated wolf, like and yet unlike all other wolves, crosses alone from smiling timber land into an open space among the trees. Here a yellow stream flows from rotted moose-hide sacks and sinks into the ground, long grasses growing through it, vegetable mould overrunning it. He muses for a time, howls once, long and mournfully, ere he departs.

But he is not always alone. When long winter nights come on and the wolves follow their meat into the lower valleys, he may be seen running at the head of the pack through the pale moonlight or glimmering borealis, leaping gigantic above his fellows, his great throat a-bellow as he sings a song of the younger world, which is the song of the pack.

FADE OUT.

THE END
