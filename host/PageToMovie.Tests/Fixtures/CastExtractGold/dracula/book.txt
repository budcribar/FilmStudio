DRACULA
Bram Stoker

CHAPTER 1
Jonathan Harker's Journal

3 May. Bistritz. — Left Munich at 8:35 P.M., on 1st May, arriving at Vienna early next morning; should have arrived at 6:46, but train was an hour late.

I found that my landlord had got a letter from the Count, directing him to secure the best place on the coach for me; but on making inquiries as to details he seemed somewhat reticent, and pretended that he could not understand my German.

4 May. — I found that Count Dracula had directed my journey in every way. When I told him that I should leave by the 10 o'clock train that night, he seemed very much disturbed.

CHAPTER 2

The Count himself came forward to meet me. He was a tall old man, clean shaven save for a long white moustache, and clad in black from head to foot, without a single speck of colour about him anywhere. He held in his hand an antique silver lamp, in which the flame burned without chimney or globe of any kind, throwing long quivering shadows as it flickered in the draught of the open door.

Later Mina Harker and Professor Van Helsing joined the struggle against the Count. Lucy Westenra was among the first to suffer his attention in England.
