--- PAGE 1 ---
THE JUNGLE BOOK
Rudyard Kipling

--- PAGE 2 ---
It was seven o'clock of a very warm evening in the Seeonee hills when Father Wolf woke up from his day's rest. Mother Wolf lay with her big gray nose dropped across her four tumbling, squealing cubs.

--- PAGE 3 ---
"Shere Khan, the Big One, has shifted his hunting grounds," said Father Wolf. "He will hunt among these hills for the next moon, so he has told me."

--- PAGE 4 ---
Mother Wolf shook herself clear of the cubs and sprang forward. "Something is coming uphill," she said. "Get ready."

A small brown baby who could just walk crawled into the cave. He looked up into Father Wolf's face and laughed.

--- PAGE 5 ---
"Is that a man's cub?" said Mother Wolf. "I have never seen one. Bring it here."

"A man's cub," said Father Wolf. "Look!"

--- PAGE 6 ---
Mother Wolf threw herself down, her eyes open, and her great gray muzzle dropped to the ground. "How little! How naked, and — how bold!" she said softly. "The man's cub was born of a free people. He shall run with the Pack. We will call him Mowgli, the Frog."

--- PAGE 7 ---
Shere Khan, the tiger with a lame foot, roared at the mouth of the cave. "The cub is mine! Give him to me!"

Father Wolf stood up. "The Wolves are a free people," he said. "They take orders from the Head of the Pack, not from a striped cattle-killer."

--- PAGE 8 ---
Years later, Mowgli stood before Akela at Council Rock. Baloo the bear and Bagheera the black panther spoke for him.

"He is a man-cub," said Akela, "but he has paid for his meat. Let him run with the Pack."
